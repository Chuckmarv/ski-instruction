About Chuck

Ski & Ride School Supervisor · PSIA Member

I've spent seasons teaching and supervising at Big Bear Mountain Resort's Ski & Ride School, working with everyone from nervous first-timers to strong skiers chasing cleaner technique.

My approach is simple: look at what your skis are doing, find the one change that makes the biggest difference, and give you a drill you can feel right away. Lessons stay relaxed, safe, and focused on mileage.

Instruction

Pick your level for the fundamentals I focus on, then try the drills next time you're on snow.

Step 1

Balance & Stance

Stand tall and athletic: ankles, knees, and hips gently flexed, hands forward where you can see them.

  • Feel your shins touching the boot tongues
  • Weight centered over the whole foot
Step 2

Wedge & Speed Control

Push your heels apart to make a small wedge. A bigger wedge slows you down; a smaller one lets you glide.

  • Practice "pizza big, pizza small"
  • Stop with a wide, calm wedge
Step 3

First Turns

Turn by gently steering both feet and legs the way you want to go, not by twisting your shoulders.

  • Look where you want to go
  • Link turns across the hill to control speed
Focus 1

Matching Your Skis

Move from a wedge to parallel by bringing the inside ski parallel earlier in each turn.

  • Shrink the wedge on easy terrain
  • Lighten the inside ski as you turn
Focus 2

Pole Plant & Rhythm

A light pole touch times your turns and keeps your upper body facing down the hill.

  • Touch, turn, touch, turn
  • Keep hands in front, no big arm swings
Focus 3

Edging Over Skidding

Tip your skis on edge with your ankles and knees so they bend and carve instead of sliding sideways.

  • Railroad-track drill on gentle groomers
  • Look back at your tracks: thin lines = edging
Refine 1

Early Edge Engagement

Start building edge angle at the top of the turn so the ski grips before the fall line, not after.

  • Roll to new edges from the feet up
  • Shape a "C" turn, not a "Z"
Refine 2

Separation & Angulation

Let your legs turn under a quiet, downhill-facing upper body, and create angles to hold the edge.

  • Hold a pole across your hips as a level
  • Outside ski does most of the work
Refine 3

Versatility

Adapt the same fundamentals to bumps, steeps, and variable snow by adjusting timing and pressure.

  • Absorb bumps with flexion and extension
  • Short-radius turns on steeper pitches
